# Walk-forward + embargo math, with the BNTX-2021 fold worked out

The stock-modeling pipeline does not use a single train/test split. It uses **purged walk-forward cross-validation** (López de Prado, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, Ch. 7), implemented in `src/stock_chart/splits.py`. For every test year, the splitter has to answer a deceptively annoying question: *exactly how much of the prior year's training data must we throw away so that no training label resolves inside the test fold?* This post works the math out concretely against one row — BNTX on **2021-07-02** with a 40-trading-day horizon — so the dates are unambiguous.

## The label-leakage problem

Anchored returns are **forward-looking labels**. An anchor on 2020-12-15 with `H=40` has its outcome decided on roughly 2021-02-10. If that row is in the training set for a fold whose test period is calendar-year 2021, then the model has *already seen* a label that was decided inside the test window. The features at the anchor look like past data, but the target lives in the future — and that future overlaps the test fold.

The model isn't memorizing test-fold features. It's worse than that: it's being trained on labels that were *generated by the test-fold price path*. Out-of-sample scores get a free boost that vanishes the moment you deploy.

## The fix in concrete steps

For test_year = **2021** the splitter computes:

```
test_start          = 2021-01-01
test_end            = 2021-12-31
horizon (H)         = 40 trading days
embargo_multiplier  = 1                          # config default
embargo_trading     = 40 * 1   = 40 trading days
embargo_calendar    = int(40 * 1.6) = 64 calendar days
cutoff              = test_start - embargo_calendar
                    = 2021-01-01 - 64 days
                    = 2020-10-29
```

The rule applied to every candidate training row is:

> Drop the row if `resolution_date_40d >= 2020-10-29`.

Important: the cutoff is on the **resolution date**, not on the anchor date. We don't ask "when was this row's anchor?" — we ask "when does this row's label become known?" That's the date that can leak into the test fold.

## What this purges, with concrete BNTX-flavored dates

Working backwards from the cutoff:

- An anchor on **2020-08-15** has `resolution_date_40d ≈ 2020-10-13`. Since 2020-10-13 < 2020-10-29, **kept**.
- An anchor on **2020-10-15** has `resolution_date_40d ≈ 2020-12-15`. Since 2020-12-15 > 2020-10-29, **purged**.
- The latest surviving resolution is around 2020-10-28. Walking back 40 trading days from there gives an anchor around **2020-09-01**.

So the practical effect:

- Training anchors **2017-01-03 through ≈2020-09-01** are kept.
- Training anchors from **≈2020-09-02 through 2020-12-31** are purged, because their forward-40d labels resolve at or after 2020-10-29.

That removes roughly the last **four calendar months** of 2020 training data — substantially more than just "a week or two of buffer." This is the cost of an embargo, and it is the right cost: any of those purged rows would have a label whose value was determined by price action that the test fold is supposed to evaluate the model against.

## BNTX-2021 specifically

BNTX has an anchor on **2021-07-02** with `H=40` → `resolution_date_40d = 2021-08-27`. Both dates sit squarely inside calendar-year 2021, so this row lives in the **test** fold for `test_year = 2021`. The model that produced a score for BNTX-2021-07-02 was therefore trained on:

- All eligible anchors from **2017-01** through **≈2020-09-01**
- Zero rows whose resolution date is on or after **2020-10-29**
- Zero rows from **2021** at all

No 2021 price information — neither as features nor as labels — touched the model that scored BNTX-2021-07-02. That is the invariant the embargo enforces.

## Why × 1.6?

The horizon is expressed in **trading days**, but the embargo gate operates on **calendar dates** (because resolution dates and test-fold boundaries are calendar dates). The conversion has to be conservative in the right direction.

40 trading days span between roughly 56 and 64 calendar days depending on weekends and holidays (Thanksgiving + Christmas weeks shorten things; a clean stretch lengthens them). If we under-estimated, a forward-40d label from late October could *just* squeak into early January and leak into the test fold by one or two days. The 1.6 multiplier (≈ 8/5, the inverse of the trading-day fraction of a week) is a deliberate slight over-estimate. The cost of being slightly too aggressive is dropping a handful of borderline training rows. The cost of being slightly too lax is silent leakage. The asymmetry favors 1.6.

## Timeline

```
                                          embargo
                                       (~4 months)
                                       <--------->
2017-01 ........ 2020-09-01            2021-01-01 ........ 2021-12-31
[========== TRAIN ==========][== PURGED ==][========= TEST =========]
                                    ^                ^
                            cutoff (resolutions    BNTX anchor
                            >= 2020-10-29 dropped)  2021-07-02
                                                    resolves 2021-08-27
```

## Verification

`splits.py` defines an `assert_no_leakage(train_df, test_start, embargo_calendar)` helper that the test suite calls for every fold. It re-derives the cutoff from `test_start - embargo_calendar` and asserts:

```python
assert (train_df["resolution_date_40d"] < cutoff).all(), (
    f"Leakage: {(train_df['resolution_date_40d'] >= cutoff).sum()} "
    f"training rows resolve at or after {cutoff}"
)
```

If any future refactor accidentally widens the training window (a common regression — someone "fixes" what looks like missing data near year-end), this assertion fails loudly at fold-construction time rather than silently inflating CV scores.
