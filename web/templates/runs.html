What is "best CAGR" really measuring?

CAGR = compound annual growth rate. It's the constant annual return that, if compounded over the test period, would produce the observed final equity. CAGR does not account for risk — a strategy with 30% CAGR and 70% drawdown is worse than one with 20% CAGR and 15% drawdown. Look at the run detail page for max drawdown and Sharpe ratio.

Within an already-momentum-prefiltered universe (the pipeline only considers stocks where Close > 1.5 × 250-day MA), the "random" baseline already inherits strong momentum exposure. So the model needs to beat random by a substantial margin, not just edge it out.
