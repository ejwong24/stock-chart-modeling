The Magic Number

An email arrived from a researcher named Greg with a specific claim: start with $100,000 in 2019, follow his stock-picking model's recommendations for 7 years, and you'd have $3.23 million today.

What the email actually said

The original document was a careful 80-page write-up of a research pipeline. It used a state-of-the-art image-recognition AI (Meta's DINOv2) to "read" stock charts and rank stocks by their visual pattern. The headline numbers were explicit:

Starting capital
$100k
Ending equity
$3.23M
CAGR
+61%
vs random
100th pct

The S&P 500 averages about 10% per year over the long run. Real-world hedge funds typically dream of 15-20% per year. Anything above 30% is a unicorn. 61% per year is nearly Madoff-grade impossible.

Why we cared

The email was sincere, methodologically transparent, and from someone the user trusts. That's the worst kind of result — one that sounds too good to be true and is also detailed enough that you can't easily dismiss it. Greg wasn't claiming magic; he was claiming a specific pipeline gave a specific result. To know whether he'd found something real, we had to reproduce it ourselves under honest conditions and see what survived.

How to read this chart

The y-axis is logarithmic, so each gridline is 10×. The original claim ($3.23M, leftmost red bar) is more than a full power-of-ten above our honest reproduction (third bar). Our best new strategy with all the realistic costs turned on (fourth bar) ends up at $256k. Random picks in the same prefiltered universe end at $295k without any model. The ML strategies are clustered with random, not with the original claim.
