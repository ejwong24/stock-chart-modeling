The Bottom Line

Seven chapters of analysis distill to one paragraph. Here it is.

The single-paragraph answer

The original document's "+61% per year, $100k → $3.23M" claim was almost certainly a methodological artifact. Reproducing the same method honestly under realistic transaction costs gives ~+5% CAGR — barely above the random baseline in the same momentum-prefiltered universe (~+11% median random CAGR). A simple non-AI rule ("rank by low 60-day volatility, hold") achieves about the same return as the AI but with half the drawdown. After deflated-Sharpe and block-bootstrap correction, the apparent edge is not statistically distinguishable from luck. The strategy might have a small real edge or no edge at all — we can't tell from one pass through the data, and the AI image-encoding pipeline does not earn its complexity.

What this means for the research effort

Greg's effort was not wasted. The pipeline, the audit, and the corrected methodology are real assets. Specifically:

  • The corrected pipeline (purged walk-forward, deflated Sharpe, block bootstrap, honest report card, lockbox protocol) is reusable for any future stock-prediction experiment.
  • The 60-subagent research deep dives (/research) lay out the five hard open problems with concrete code recommendations: survivorship-bias closure, encoder bake-off, lockbox protocol, transaction-cost calibration, path-dependent exits.
  • The SMA250 systems-comparison test establishes a baseline that any future model comparison can be checked against.
  • The simple low-volatility momentum rule (rank_inv_60d_vol) is a real, deployable strategy with 12% CAGR, −38% max DD, and Sharpe 0.61. It needed no AI.

What would change our minds?

The five things that would make us update toward "this works"

  1. A frozen 2025 lockbox: predict 2025 picks using only 2024-and-earlier data, see the result for the first time on 2026-01-01. If the lockbox confirms the in-sample numbers within the noise band, that's evidence of real signal.
  2. A pre-registered single configuration (not best-of-72) running forward for 6+ months in a paper-trading account. If the realized fills show the simulator's bps estimates were right, that's evidence the strategy can be implemented.
  3. Survivorship-corrected data: rerun the whole thing on a delisted-history-inclusive dataset (Polygon $30/mo or EODHD $20/mo). If the corrected CAGR is in the same ballpark as our current numbers, the strategy survives that filter.
  4. A successful comparison against simple-momentum baselines: explicitly demonstrate that the AI strategy beats engineered features by >2 percentage points of Sharpe ratio, consistently across folds, with non-overlapping confidence intervals.
  5. Replication by Greg's system (or any independent system) under matching cost assumptions on the SMA250 baseline test. If the medians agree within tolerance, the foundations are common; if not, that gap is where to debug.
