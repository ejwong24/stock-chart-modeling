The Systems-Comparison Test

Before you compare two backtesting systems on a fancy model, you have to know they agree on the same trivial baseline. Greg wrote a separate spec to nail this down. Here's what we found running it.

Why this exists

Backtesting frameworks are like translators — subtly different conventions about "close price" (raw vs split-adjusted), anchor dates (Friday vs last-trading-day), share counts (fractional vs integer), and date handling can produce numerically different answers from the same data. When two researchers disagree on a model's CAGR, it's often because their baseline framework disagrees, not because of the model.

Greg's spec defines a dead-simple baseline:

  • Filter: Close > 1.5 × 250-day moving average
  • Selection: uniform random from eligible stocks (no model)
  • Buy: up to 5 random stocks each week, 4% of equity each
  • Hold: exactly 20 or 40 trading days
  • Costs: zero
  • Seeds: 200 (numbered 0 through 199)

If two systems agree on the medians + p5 + p95 across 200 seeds, they agree on the data plumbing. If they disagree, you compare the trade lists to find where.

What this tells us about the model

The H=40 median random portfolio achieved +16.35% CAGR in this universe. Greg's original model claimed +61% — that's nearly 4× the random median. Any system that produces a model number 4× the random median in a momentum-prefiltered universe should be examined very carefully — that gap is far larger than the noise band the random seeds define.

How the two systems can disagree (the debugging guide)

If Greg's system produces materially different medians, the spec specifies the order of investigation:

  1. Universe mismatch. Different sets of tickers eligible on the same date. Easy to test: compare the ticker list on, say, 2019-01-04.
  2. Price-adjustment mismatch. Raw close vs split-adjusted close. Look at a known split date and compare prices.
  3. Date mismatch. Friday vs Thursday anchors, holiday handling. Compare a holiday week.
  4. Share-count mismatch. Fractional vs integer. Our system uses integer floor; declared.
  5. Accounting mismatch. When does compounding start? What's the equity definition?

The first 50 trades from H=20, seed=0

The spec section 12 says: "if the systems disagree, compare the first 50 trades of seed 0 trade-by-trade. That will quickly reveal whether the difference is universe, prices, dates, or accounting."

What this enables

Once two systems agree on the SMA250 random baseline, you can run any new model on both and trust the comparison. Without that baseline-agreement, every model-vs-model comparison is contaminated by data-plumbing differences.

This is the kind of methodological discipline most academic papers skip. Greg's spec was exactly right.
