Reality Bites: Costs

Every time you buy or sell a stock, you lose a tiny bit to spread, slippage, commission, and (eventually) taxes. For the microcap names this strategy loves, those costs are huge. Here's what happened when we turned them on.

Why costs matter so much here

The strategy buys ~5 stocks every week and holds for 40 trading days. That's roughly 6 round-trip turns per year. Each round-trip in a small-cap costs anywhere from 20 to 200 basis points (0.2% to 2%) depending on:

  • Bid-ask spread. You buy at the higher price, sell at the lower. For microcaps the spread is often 50-200 bps just sitting there before you trade.
  • Market impact. When your order is a noticeable fraction of a stock's daily volume, the price moves against you. Big sellers go up. Big buyers push the price up. This scales as the square root of your participation rate — a well-studied model called Almgren-Chriss.
  • Halts. Microcaps frequently get halted for news. About 2% of microcap trade attempts fail outright.
  • Trailing stops. We added an 18% trailing stop (exit if price drops 18% from peak since buying). This reduces drawdowns but cuts some winners short.

The biggest casualty

The DINOv2 image stack lost 10 percentage points of CAGR to realistic costs — more than any other strategy. Why? It picks the most volatile, most thinly-traded microcaps where impact is worst. Its "alpha" was being eaten alive by the very stocks it loved.

Capacity ceiling — and why this strategy can't scale

The numbers above are for a $100k account. As your account grows, your $4,000 positions become $40,000 positions ($1M AUM), then $400,000 positions ($10M AUM). But the median candidate stock has only ~$1.4M of daily trading volume. A $400k buy is 28% of daily volume — you'd move the price against yourself by hundreds of basis points.

And these are pre-tax numbers. A 40-day-hold strategy realizes 100% short-term capital gains, which a high-bracket US individual will lose ~38-42% of. The $100k account's +14% becomes maybe +8% post-tax.

The pattern emerging

Every honest correction we apply makes the strategy look worse. We're closing in on the actual answer: this is a high-noise, high-cost ML pipeline whose apparent edge is mostly methodological artifact. But we haven't yet asked the most important question — does the AI part actually help? Next chapter has the falsification test.
