The Honest Reproduction

We built the whole pipeline from scratch in ~3,700 lines of Python, then ran it on 3,429 US stocks across 8 walk-forward folds with 200 random baselines for comparison. When we faithfully reproduced the original method, the magic number collapsed.

What we actually built

The pipeline has 8 stages, each a small Python module that does one thing well:

  1. Universe — download list of US common stocks
  2. Data — pull 10 years of daily prices from yfinance (with split adjustments turned on)
  3. Labels — for every Friday, label "did this stock go up 25% in the next 40 trading days? Y/N"
  4. Features — compute 40 numbers per (stock, Friday) summarizing the past year
  5. Render + Embed — also draw a 224×224 chart image and run it through DINOv2
  6. Splits — carefully split history into "study these years, predict that year" chunks with no leakage
  7. Models — train 3 prediction models per chunk (LR original, LightGBM-with-images, LightGBM-engineered-only)
  8. Simulator — pretend to actually buy/sell based on predictions; compare to random and to 5 simple non-AI rules

The fixes from the audit went into stages 3-6: purged walk-forward with embargo in splits.py kills label leakage; the 3 model tracks let us A/B-test whether the images add value over engineered features.

The first big result: 8 strategies, 7 years, no realistic costs yet

The shock

Our reproduction of the original method (the lr_baseline row above, +11.47% CAGR) beats the random median by just 13 basis points per year. The model is essentially indistinguishable from random selection within the same momentum-prefiltered universe. The 61% CAGR from the original paper was driven by some combination of survivorship bias, multiple-comparison cherry-picking, walk-forward label leakage, and the absence of cost modeling — not by the model finding real signal.

Why did the simple non-AI strategies do so badly on this run?

The two big losers (rank_60d_return at −2.4% and rank_ma250_extension at −4.4%) are pure momentum chasers. They buy the most extreme recent winners. In a universe that's already prefiltered for momentum, doubling down on the most-extended names just rides the biggest bubbles down. They're not strategies; they're cautionary tales about why you need ranking subtlety, not just "buy what went up."

The de facto winner on risk-adjusted basis — the strategy with the best Sharpe (0.61) and the smallest drawdown (−38%) — is rank_inv_60d_vol: "buy the calmest stocks within the momentum universe." It's a 5-line non-AI rule.

What we've shown so far

Under honest methodology — right baselines, right splits, right random comparisons — the AI strategy is no better than picking at random in the same prefiltered universe. But we haven't yet added real-world trading costs. Those make it worse. Next chapter.
