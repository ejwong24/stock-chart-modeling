📈 Stock Chart Modeling — Corrected Rebuild

This is a from-scratch re-implementation of a stock-selection research pipeline, with explicit corrections for 7 high-severity flaws identified by a multi-subagent audit of the original design document. You can use this UI without writing any code.

What does this pipeline actually do? It tries to predict which stocks will go up the most over the next 5–40 trading days, by looking at the shape of their price chart over the past year and their volume pattern. It then runs a portfolio simulator that actually buys/sells based on the predictions and reports how much money you would have made versus picking stocks at random.

Why is the "corrected rebuild" different from the original? The original document claimed 61% annualized returns. After honest statistical correction it's likely 14–17% net of trading costs — and that may not actually beat a simple "rank by 12-month momentum" strategy. See Flaws & Fixes for the full critique.

What does each box on this page mean?

Universe size: the candidate ticker pool fetched from NASDAQ. Active US listings minus ETFs and test issues, plus a hand-curated delisted-ticker seed list.

OHLCV data on disk: how many of those tickers have daily Open/High/Low/Close/Volume parquet files cached locally (downloaded from yfinance with split-adjustment turned on).

Completed runs: finished pipeline runs. Each run has its own folder under reports/ containing equity curves, trade blotters, and a headline.json summary.

Active background runs: if you launched a run from the UI, it shows up here while the Python process is alive.
